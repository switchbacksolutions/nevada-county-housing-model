# Nevada County population and home values: model results

All prices are real 2025 dollars (CPI-U deflated). Generated by `model.py`.

## Stage 1: population and real home-price growth, peer counties

Dependent variable: annual log change in each county's real FHFA house price index.
Year fixed effects absorb statewide and national drivers such as mortgage rates.

Headline set: the 30 California counties with mean population under 200,000, the set that includes Nevada County (102,000).
27 of them have price data.
Large counties show a much larger association (see specification curve).

27 counties, years 2001-2024, n = 648. County and year fixed effects. Standard errors clustered by county and year.

## Local projections

Each row is a separate fit: the log real price change from the year before a population change through
the end of year n, where year 1 is the year of the change itself, on that year's population growth, with
the same controls and county and year fixed effects. Each fit also holds population growth in the later
years of the window fixed, so the value is the cumulative price change in percent for a one-year 1%
population change with later growth unchanged. Intervals come from 1,000 county block bootstrap
resamples; every horizon is refit on the same resample.

## Long-difference check

Log real price change on log population change over non-overlapping windows, with window-end year
effects and standard errors clustered by county.
The 10-year long-difference coefficient measures the same horizon without the momentum, income and permit controls, over two non-overlapping windows per county.

| Window (years) | Peer counties (s.e.) | Windows | All counties (s.e.) | Windows |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.69 (0.16) | 675 | 1.07 (0.18) | 1,375 |
| 5 | 0.52 (0.19) | 135 | 0.87 (0.19) | 275 |
| 10 | 0.15 (0.16) | 54 | 0.59 (0.19) | 110 |

Applied to a sustained 10-year path, the sum of the year 1 to 10 responses implies 1.18
for peer counties and 2.25 for all counties, against 10-year long-difference coefficients of
0.15 and 0.59.

## Out-of-sample check

For each origin year from 2014 on, the model is fit on earlier years only. It then predicts each county's
cumulative real price change over the next 1 and 5 years, using the county's actual later population, income and permit
data. Predictions and actual changes are both measured as deviations from the mean of the counties in that sample for that origin, because
year effects are unknown ahead of time. RMSE is in log points. The `zero` model predicts no deviation for every county.
Pairs are the pooled county-origin pairs.

| Years ahead | Model | Peer RMSE | Peer correlation | Peer pairs | All-county RMSE | All-county correlation | All-county pairs |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | full | 0.027 | 0.14 | 270 | 0.026 | 0.34 | 550 |
| 1 | no_population | 0.027 | 0.02 | 270 | 0.027 | 0.19 | 550 |
| 1 | zero | 0.026 | n/a | 270 | 0.026 | n/a | 550 |
| 5 | full | 0.063 | 0.31 | 162 | 0.085 | 0.38 | 330 |
| 5 | no_population | 0.063 | 0.20 | 162 | 0.086 | -0.05 | 330 |
| 5 | zero | 0.060 | n/a | 162 | 0.074 | n/a | 330 |

This tests whether population growth helps rank counties' price growth ahead of time. It is not evidence of causation.

## Placebo check

These fits use all counties.

Next year's population growth, which this year's prices cannot respond to, is included as a
regressor. If it predicts this year's price growth, the population coefficient reflects shared
local shocks (jobs, amenities, remote work) rather than a timing story.

Next year's population growth on its own:

| Term | Coefficient | 90% CI | p-value |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Last year's real price growth | 0.376 | 0.229 to 0.522 | 0.000 |
| Next year's population growth (placebo) | 0.928 | 0.540 to 1.316 | 0.000 |
| Real per-capita income growth | -0.035 | -0.141 to 0.072 | 0.590 |
| Last year's permits per 1,000 residents | -0.001 | -0.002 to 0.001 | 0.437 |

55 counties, years 2001-2024, n = 1320. County and year fixed effects. Standard errors clustered by county and year.

Neither timing choice is a bound on a causal effect. Both are associations.

combined with the local-projection response at every later horizon. Every other driver stays
