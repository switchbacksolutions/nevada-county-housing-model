Nevada County, California · Housing model

When more people move to Nevada County, what happens to home prices in Grass Valley and Nevada City?

This model uses 24 years of data from California counties to measure how population growth changes home values. It then applies that result to the two cities. All dollar values are in dollars, adjusted for inflation.

25 years of population and prices

Population grew slowly and steadily. Home values swung much more, with the 2000s housing bubble, the 2008 crash and the pandemic jump in 2020-2021. Population growth is one cause among several, which is why the model needs data from many counties to measure it. Markers show events that coincided with turns in the market. They are dates, not causes.

Prices keep rising for a few years after people arrive

Prices keep moving for a few years after people arrive. The direct estimate measures the price change one, two, up to ten years after a one-year population change, horizon by horizon. The momentum model extends a one-year relation. They agree at short horizons; where they part, trust the direct estimate.

How the model works

  1. Population effect across California. County effects remove fixed local differences. Year effects remove things that hit every county at once, such as mortgage rates and recessions.
  2. County to city. Each city's Zillow Home Value Index is compared with the Nevada County price index for 2002-.
  3. Scenarios. Each year of a constant population growth rate is combined with the direct estimate of the price change at every later horizon, for years. The shaded range shows 90% of 1,000 county bootstrap resamples, combined with draws of the city pass-through coefficient.

Two estimates, and why they differ

Population and prices move together, and the data cannot say which moves first. The same-year estimate pairs this year's population change with this year's price change. The lagged estimate pairs last year's population change with this year's price change. A placebo check shows that next year's population change predicts this year's prices nearly as well, so neither number is a causal effect or a bound on one. Both are associations under a timing choice.

Out-of-sample check

For each origin year from on, the model is fit on earlier years only and predicts each county's price change over the next 1 and 5 years, as a deviation from the mean of the counties in that sample. The zero row predicts no deviation for every county. RMSE is in log points. Pairs are the pooled county-origin pairs. This tests whether population growth helps rank counties' price growth ahead of time. It is not evidence of causation.

Limits

  • The model treats a population decline as the mirror image of growth. The data show no reliable difference between growth and decline.
  • Census has no corrected 2010-2020 county population series. The model spreads the gap between the 2010s estimates and the 2020 Census over 2011-2019.
  • Zillow city values cover all home types at the middle of the market. Individual neighborhoods and home types can move differently.

Sources

  • Home prices: FHFA all-transactions house price index by county, and the Zillow Home Value Index for each city.
  • Population: U.S. Census Bureau county population estimates.
  • Income and building permits: Bureau of Economic Analysis and Census Bureau, through FRED.
  • Inflation: Consumer Price Index for all urban consumers (CPI-U).
